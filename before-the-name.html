Before the Name

Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti was born Frances Abigail Olufunmilayo Thomas on October 25, 1900, in Abeokuta, in what is now Ogun State, Nigeria. She was the daughter of Daniel Olumoyewa Thomas, a farmer and trader, and Phyllis Moyeni Dese, a dressmaker. Her parents were educated Christians who placed importance on education and had high hopes for their children. She was born into a Yoruba family at a time when Nigeria was under British colonial rule, and the opportunities available to African women were deeply limited. Yet Abeokuta was a town with a rich history, shaped by Yoruba culture, trade, community life and the determination of its people. Women played important roles in the local economy, particularly through trading, supporting their families and participating in community affairs. Growing up in this environment meant that Funmilayo's early world was shaped by both tradition and change. Family values, cultural expectations and the growing influence of colonial rule formed part of the society into which she was born. Although she had not yet begun the journey that would make her a prominent political activist, the circumstances of her time provide an important backdrop to understanding her life. Her name at birth was Frances Abigail Olufunmilayo Thomas. The name Funmilayo would become widely associated with a woman who challenged injustice and demanded greater rights for Nigerian women. But before the public speeches, the protests and the political struggles, there was a young girl whose story began within a family in Abeokuta. The girl who would later become known as Funmilayo Ransome-Kuti therefore did not begin her life as a political figure. She began as a daughter, a student and a young woman growing up in Abeokuta, long before her name became associated with women's rights and political resistance. Her journey from these beginnings to national prominence is a story that unfolds through the experiences, choices and education that shaped the woman she would become.
